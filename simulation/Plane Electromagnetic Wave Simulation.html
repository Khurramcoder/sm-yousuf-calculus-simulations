Mathematical Background

Maxwell's Equations in Vacuum:

∇·E = 0, ∇·B = 0, ∇×E = -∂B/∂t, ∇×B = μ₀ε₀ ∂E/∂t

Wave Equations:

∇²E - (1/c²) ∂²E/∂t² = 0, ∇²B - (1/c²) ∂²B/∂t² = 0

Plane Wave Solutions:

E(r,t) = E₀ exp[i(k·r - ωt)], B(r,t) = B₀ exp[i(k·r - ωt)]

Conducting Medium:

∇²E - εμ ∂²E/∂t² - μσ ∂E/∂t = 0

Transverse Nature

Electromagnetic waves are transverse: both E and B fields are perpendicular to the direction of propagation.

Mutual Perpendicularity

The E and B fields are perpendicular to each other, forming a right-handed coordinate system with the propagation direction.

Phase Relationship

In vacuum, E and B oscillate in phase. In conductors, they may have a phase difference due to attenuation.

Wave Impedance

The ratio |E|/|B| equals the speed of light in vacuum, and the wave impedance in a medium is Z = √(μ/ε).
