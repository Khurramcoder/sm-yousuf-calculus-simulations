Setting up the patch

A surface written as a height function

A Monge patch describes a surface as the graph of a function: every point is displaced above a plane by an amount f(u,v). Fixing this simple form lets every geometric quantity — tangents, normal, curvature — collapse to derivatives of one scalar function.

$$\mathbf{x}(u,v) = u\,\mathbf{e}_1 + v\,\mathbf{e}_2 + f(u,v)\,\mathbf{e}_3$$

Differentiating twice introduces five shorthand quantities that carry the whole computation:

$$p=f_u \qquad q=f_v$$
$$r=f_{uu} \qquad s=f_{uv} \qquad t=f_{vv}$$

Because the base coordinates u, v are themselves independent directions, the tangent vectors are immediate:

$$\mathbf{x}_u=\mathbf{e}_1+p\,\mathbf{e}_3 \qquad \mathbf{x}_v=\mathbf{e}_2+q\,\mathbf{e}_3 \qquad \mathbf{x}_{uu}=r\,\mathbf{e}_3 \qquad \mathbf{x}_{uv}=s\,\mathbf{e}_3 \qquad \mathbf{x}_{vv}=t\,\mathbf{e}_3$$
First fundamental form

How the patch measures length

Dotting the tangent vectors together gives the metric coefficients E, F, G, and their determinant defines the single scalar g that will appear under every square root from here on.

Metric coefficients

$$E=\mathbf{x}_u\!\cdot\!\mathbf{x}_u=1+p^2$$ $$F=\mathbf{x}_u\!\cdot\!\mathbf{x}_v=pq$$ $$G=\mathbf{x}_v\!\cdot\!\mathbf{x}_v=1+q^2$$

Determinant & unit normal

$$g \equiv EG-F^2 = 1+p^2+q^2$$ $$\mathbf{N}=\frac{\mathbf{x}_u\times\mathbf{x}_v}{|\mathbf{x}_u\times\mathbf{x}_v|}=\frac{-(p\,\mathbf{e}_1+q\,\mathbf{e}_2-\mathbf{e}_3)}{\sqrt{g}}$$
Second fundamental form

How the patch bends away from its tangent plane

Projecting the second derivatives onto the unit normal N measures curvature directly — each coefficient is just r, s, t rescaled by 1/√g:

$$L=\mathbf{x}_{uu}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{N}=\dfrac{r}{\sqrt{g}}$$
$$M=\mathbf{x}_{uv}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{N}=\dfrac{s}{\sqrt{g}}$$
$$\mathcal{N}=\mathbf{x}_{vv}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{N}=\dfrac{t}{\sqrt{g}}$$

Two more derivatives — of E, F, G themselves — will feed the Christoffel symbols below:

$$E_u=2pr \qquad E_v=2ps \qquad F_u=ps+qr \qquad F_v=pt+qs \qquad G_u=2qs \qquad G_v=2qt$$
Christoffel symbols & Weingarten coefficients

The two ingredients of a moving frame

Every derivative of the frame {xu, xv, N} can be re-expressed in terms of the frame itself. The tangential part is governed by the Christoffel symbols Γ; the way the normal tilts is governed by the Weingarten coefficients β. For a Monge patch both reduce to clean rational expressions in p, q, r, s, t:

Christoffel symbols

$$\Gamma^1_{11}=\dfrac{pr}{g}\quad \Gamma^1_{12}=\dfrac{ps}{g}\quad \Gamma^1_{22}=\dfrac{pt}{g}$$ $$\Gamma^2_{11}=\dfrac{qr}{g}\quad \Gamma^2_{12}=\dfrac{qs}{g}\quad \Gamma^2_{22}=\dfrac{qt}{g}$$

Weingarten coefficients

$$\beta^1_1=\dfrac{spq-rq^2-r}{g^{3/2}} \qquad \beta^1_2=\dfrac{tpq-sq^2-s}{g^{3/2}}$$ $$\beta^2_1=\dfrac{rpq-sp^2-s}{g^{3/2}} \qquad \beta^2_2=\dfrac{spq-tp^2-t}{g^{3/2}}$$
Assembling the result

The Gauss–Weingarten equations

Substituting E, F, G, L, M, 𝒩 into the general Gauss and Weingarten formulas, and multiplying through by g, every coefficient collapses to the same handful of terms — p, q, r, s, t and g — closing the derivation.

$$g\,\mathbf{x}_{uu}=pr\,\mathbf{x}_u+qr\,\mathbf{x}_v+r\,g^{1/2}\mathbf{N}$$ $$g\,\mathbf{x}_{uv}=ps\,\mathbf{x}_u+qs\,\mathbf{x}_v+s\,g^{1/2}\mathbf{N}$$ $$g\,\mathbf{x}_{vv}=pt\,\mathbf{x}_u+qt\,\mathbf{x}_v+t\,g^{1/2}\mathbf{N}$$
$$g^{3/2}\mathbf{N}_u=(spq-rq^2-r)\,\mathbf{x}_u+(rpq-sp^2-s)\,\mathbf{x}_v$$ $$g^{3/2}\mathbf{N}_v=(tpq-sq^2-s)\,\mathbf{x}_u+(spq-tp^2-t)\,\mathbf{x}_v$$

Each line is exactly the Gauss formula xij = Γkijxk + LijN or Weingarten formula Ni = βkixk, multiplied through by g (or g3/2) to clear the denominators derived above.
