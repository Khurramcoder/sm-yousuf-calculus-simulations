Legendre Polynomials

Legendre polynomials are solutions to Legendre's differential equation:

(1 - x²)y'' - 2xy' + n(n+1)y = 0

where n is a non-negative integer.

These polynomials form an orthogonal system on the interval [-1, 1] and have numerous applications in physics and engineering, particularly in problems with spherical symmetry.

First Few Legendre Polynomials:

P₀(x) = 1

P₁(x) = x

P₂(x) = ½(3x² - 1)

P₃(x) = ½(5x³ - 3x)

P₄(x) = ⅛(35x⁴ - 30x² + 3)

P₅(x) = ⅛(63x⁵ - 70x³ + 15x)

Example: Expressing a Polynomial in Legendre Basis

Express f(x) = x⁴ + 2x³ + 2x² - x - 3 in terms of Legendre polynomials.

Using the expressions above, we can write:

f(x) = (8/35)P₄(x) + (4/5)P₃(x) + (40/21)P₂(x) + (1/5)P₁(x) - (224/105)P₀(x)

Properties of Legendre Polynomials

Orthogonality

Legendre polynomials are orthogonal on the interval [-1, 1]:

∫ Pₘ(x)Pₙ(x) dx = 0 if m ≠ n
∫ [Pₙ(x)]² dx = 2/(2n+1)

Recurrence Relations

Legendre polynomials satisfy several recurrence relations:

(2n+1)xPₙ(x) = (n+1)Pₙ₊₁(x) + nPₙ₋₁(x)
nPₙ(x) = xPₙ'(x) - Pₙ₋₁'(x)

Rodrigues' Formula

Legendre polynomials can be expressed as:

Pₙ(x) = 1/(2ⁿn!) dⁿ/dxⁿ [(x²-1)ⁿ]

Generating Function

The generating function for Legendre polynomials is:

(1 - 2xh + h²)⁻¹ᐟ² = Σ hⁿPₙ(x)

Special Values

Pₙ(1) = 1

Pₙ(-1) = (-1)ⁿ

P₂ₙ₊₁(0) = 0

P₂ₙ(0) = (-1)ⁿ(2n)!/(2²ⁿ(n!)²)

Laplace's Integrals

Laplace's first integral:

Pₙ(x) = 1/π ∫ [x ± √(x²-1)cosφ]ⁿ dφ

Laplace's second integral:

Pₙ(x) = 1/π ∫ dφ/[x ± √(x²-1)cosφ]ⁿ⁺¹

Applications of Legendre Polynomials

Physics - Electrostatics

In problems with spherical symmetry, such as the electric potential around a charged sphere, solutions to Laplace's equation are expressed in terms of Legendre polynomials.

Quantum Mechanics

The angular part of the wavefunction for the hydrogen atom involves Legendre polynomials, specifically in the spherical harmonics.

Geophysics

Legendre polynomials are used in representing the Earth's gravitational field and in geodesy.

Signal Processing

In some filter design problems, Legendre polynomials are used due to their orthogonal properties.

Example: Solving Laplace's Equation in Spherical Coordinates

In spherical coordinates (r, θ, φ), Laplace's equation ∇²V = 0 has solutions of the form:

V(r, θ) = Σ [Aₙrⁿ + Bₙr⁻⁽ⁿ⁺¹⁾] Pₙ(cosθ)

where Pₙ(cosθ) are Legendre polynomials.
