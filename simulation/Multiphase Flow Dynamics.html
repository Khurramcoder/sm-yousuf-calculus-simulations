Multiphase Flow Theory

Multiphase flows consist of two or more distinct phases (solid, liquid, gas) with characteristic fluid properties. Unlike single-phase flows, multiphase systems require specialized equations and closure models.

∂(φαρα)/∂t + ∇·(φαραuα) = Γα

This simulation demonstrates particle tracking methods where solid particles are tracked through a fluid medium, accounting for drag forces, gravity, and fluid turbulence.

Particle Motion Equation

The motion of particles in a fluid is governed by the Basset-Boussinesq-Oseen (BBO) equation:

mp dv/dt = Fdrag + Fpressure + Fadded mass + FBasset + Fbuoyancy

In this simulation, we focus on the dominant Stokes drag force and gravitational effects:

Fdrag = 6πrpμ(u - v)

Simulation Parameters

Particle Density: Controls the number of particles in the simulation. Higher density increases particle-particle interactions.

Fluid Viscosity: Affects the drag force on particles. Higher viscosity slows particle movement.

Flow Intensity: Controls the strength of the background fluid flow field.

Gravity Effect: Simulates gravitational forces pulling particles downward.
