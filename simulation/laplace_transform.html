Visual Theory

The Complex Plane

The Laplace transform maps a time-domain function $f(t)$ to a complex function $F(s)$ where $s = \sigma + i\omega$. The 3D surface shows the magnitude $|F(s)|$ as height.

σ (Real Axis)

Controls exponential damping envelope $e^{-\sigma t}$. More negative $\sigma$ = faster decay.

ω (Imaginary Axis)

Controls rotational frequency $e^{i\omega t}$. Oscillation rate in rad/s.

Poles & Zeros

×

Poles (Resonance)

Where denominator = 0. The surface shoots to infinity. Poles are the "sources" of the system's natural response.

○

Zeros (Attenuation)

Where numerator = 0. The surface flattens to zero. Zeros block certain frequencies.

1. Linearity

$\mathcal{L}\{af(t) + bg(t)\} = aF(s) + bG(s)$

Physical: The transform preserves scaling and addition.

3D Effect: Surfaces add vertically; scaling stretches height.

2. Frequency Shift

$\mathcal{L}\{e^{at}f(t)\} = F(s-a)$

Physical: Multiplying by $e^{at}$ shifts the spectrum.

3D Effect: Entire 3D mesh translates along the real $\sigma$ axis by $a$.

3. Time Shift

$\mathcal{L}\{f(t-a)u(t-a)\} = e^{-as}F(s)$

Physical: Delaying a signal introduces phase rotation.

3D Effect: Adds helical phase twist; magnitude surface unchanged.

4. Time Differentiation

$\mathcal{L}\{f'(t)\} = sF(s) - f(0^-)$

Physical: Differentiation amplifies high frequencies.

3D Effect: Surface tilts steeper; zero at origin becomes pole-like.

5. Integration

$\mathcal{L}\{\int_0^t f(\tau)d\tau\} = \frac{F(s)}{s}$

Physical: Integration smooths; adds memory.

3D Effect: Adds pole at origin; surface broadens and flattens.

6. Convolution

$\mathcal{L}\{f * g\} = F(s) \cdot G(s)$

Physical: Time-domain convolution = s-domain product.

3D Effect: Poles/zeros of both systems combine on surface.
