THE MATHEMATICS

Why a spinning top doesn't fall

A top resting on the ground under gravity is a rigid body free to rotate about a fixed point O. Gravity exerts a moment about that point, yet instead of toppling over, the top's axis sweeps out a cone. The six steps below derive that behavior from Euler's equations of rigid-body rotation, expressed in the three Euler angles — spin ψ, precession φ, and nutation θ — that the simulation above is integrating live.

I§30.1

Euler angles and angular velocity

Orient the top with three sequential rotations: precession φ about the fixed vertical Z axis, nutation θ tipping the spin axis away from vertical, and spin ψ about the body's own symmetry axis z. Projected onto the instantaneous body axes x, y, z, the three angle-rates combine — non-orthogonally — into the total angular velocity:

Eq. 1–3
$$\begin{aligned} \omega_x &= \dot\phi\sin\theta\sin\psi + \dot\theta\cos\psi \\ \omega_y &= \dot\phi\sin\theta\cos\psi - \dot\theta\sin\psi \\ \omega_z &= \dot\phi\cos\theta + \dot\psi \end{aligned}$$

These are the raw components before we exploit the top's symmetry — used again in the Lagrangian derivation in Step V.

II§30.1

Euler's equations in a frame that isn't quite body-fixed

For a symmetric top, Ixx = Iyy = I₀ and Izz = I. Angular momentum conservation, Ḣ = applied moment, must be written in a rotating frame to keep the inertia tensor constant — but we don't have to rotate with the full body spin. Because the top is axisymmetric, it's legal to rotate the axes only with φ̇ and θ̇, letting the spin ψ̇ slip relative to the frame. That frame's angular velocity is Ω, distinct from the body's own ω:

Eq. 12–17 — the moving (not spinning) frame
$$\Omega_x=\dot\theta,\qquad \Omega_y=\dot\phi\sin\theta,\qquad \Omega_z=\dot\phi\cos\theta$$ $$\omega_x=\dot\theta,\qquad \omega_y=\dot\phi\sin\theta,\qquad \omega_z=\dot\phi\cos\theta+\dot\psi$$

With Ḣ = ∂H/∂t + Ω × H and H = (I₀ωx, I₀ωy, Iωz), the moment balance M = Ḣ − HΩ + HΩ reduces, after the cross product is expanded, to three coupled nonlinear ODEs:

Eq. 27–29 — the general equations of motion
$$\begin{aligned} M_x &= I_0(\ddot\theta - \dot\phi^2\sin\theta\cos\theta) + I\dot\phi\sin\theta(\dot\phi\cos\theta+\dot\psi)\\ M_y &= I_0(\ddot\phi\sin\theta + 2\dot\phi\dot\theta\cos\theta) - I\dot\theta(\dot\phi\cos\theta+\dot\psi)\\ M_z &= I(\ddot\psi+\ddot\phi\cos\theta-\dot\phi\dot\theta\sin\theta) \end{aligned}$$

For a top pivoting on the ground, gravity supplies only Mx = Mg zG sin θ, with My = Mz = 0 — there is no moment trying to twist the spin or drag the precession directly.

III§30.2

Steady precession — the gyroscope limit

Suppose the top holds a constant tilt: θ̇ = θ̈ = φ̈ = 0, with the spin folded into a constant φ̇. Substituting into Mx above, sin θ cancels entirely, and — dropping the small φ̇² term valid whenever the spin vastly exceeds the precession rate — we're left with a strikingly simple relation:

Eq. 34–35 — steady precession rate
$$I\dot\phi\dot\psi - (I_0-I)\dot\phi^2\cos\theta = Mgz_G \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \dot\phi \approx \frac{Mgz_G}{I\dot\psi}$$

Faster spin ψ̇ means slower precession φ̇ — this is why a fast top precesses lazily while a slowing top's precession visibly speeds up. Try the Steady precession preset above: the tilt angle barely drifts because φ̇ is set to exactly this value.

IV§30.3

The general case: nutation and two conserved momenta

Drop the steady assumption and let all three angles vary. Because My = Mz = 0, two combinations of the equations of motion integrate directly into constants — generalized momenta conjugate to φ and ψ:

conjugate to precession
$$p_\phi = I_0\sin^2\theta\,\dot\phi + I\cos\theta(\dot\psi+\dot\phi\cos\theta)$$
conjugate to spin
$$p_\psi = I(\dot\psi+\dot\phi\cos\theta)$$

Solving these for φ̇ and ψ̇ in terms of θ alone collapses the entire problem to a single second-order equation for the nutation angle:

Eq. 44 — the governing equation the simulator integrates
$$\ddot\theta \;+\; \frac{\left(\dfrac{p_\phi}{I_0}-\dfrac{p_\psi}{I}\cos\theta\right)\left(\dfrac{p_\psi}{I}-\dfrac{p_\phi}{I_0}\cos\theta\right)}{\sin^3\theta} \;-\; \frac{Mgz_G}{I_0} \;=\; 0$$

This single equation, integrated numerically with a 4th-order Runge–Kutta scheme (exactly what runs behind the simulation above), produces the classic family of motions: unidirectional precession if the top is launched already precessing, cuspidal motion with sharp cusps if released from rest, and looping precession if it's launched precessing backward relative to the eventual mean drift.

V§30.4

The same result, faster — Lagrange's equation

The two conserved momenta above are not a coincidence of Euler's equations; they fall out immediately once the problem is written as a Lagrangian. With kinetic energy built from the raw ω components of Step I and potential energy V = MgzGcos θ:

the Lagrangian
$$\mathcal{L} = \tfrac12 I_0\!\left(\dot\phi^2\sin^2\theta+\dot\theta^2\right) + \tfrac12 I\left(\dot\phi\cos\theta+\dot\psi\right)^2 - Mgz_G\cos\theta$$

Since neither φ nor ψ appears explicitly in 𝓛 — only their rates do — both are cyclic coordinates, and Lagrange's equation hands back pφ = ∂𝓛/∂φ̇ and pψ = ∂𝓛/∂ψ̇ as constants of the motion for free, without ever expanding a triple cross product. Total energy E = T + V is conserved alongside them.

VI§30.5

The extreme limit: gyroscopes as instruments

When the spin ω is an order of magnitude larger than every other rotation rate in the system — the regime engineers actually call a gyroscope — the angular momentum vector locks essentially onto the spin axis, H ≈ Iωk̂. Mount such a spinning disk in gimbals on a rotating platform, and the only moment-free response left is a slow tilt θ that balances the platform's own rotation Ω against the disk's locked-in momentum:

Eq. 59 — the gyrocompass equation
$$I_0\ddot\theta + I\omega\Omega\sin\theta = 0$$

This is a pendulum equation. With a trace of friction, θ settles to zero — the spin axis aligns itself with the platform's own axis of rotation. Mount the device on the Earth instead of a turntable, and it settles pointing along the Earth's rotation axis: true north, with no compass needle or magnetic field required. This is the operating principle of the gyrocompass.
