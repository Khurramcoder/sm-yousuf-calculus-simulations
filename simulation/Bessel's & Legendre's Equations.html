Introduction to Special Functions

Many linear differential equations having variable coefficients cannot be solved by usual methods and we need to employ series solution method to find their solutions in terms of infinite convergent series.

What are Special Functions?

Special functions are particular mathematical functions that have more or less established names and notations due to their importance in mathematical analysis, functional analysis, geometry, physics, or other applications.

Bessel's Equation

The differential equation:

\[ x^2 \frac{d^2 y}{dx^2} + x \frac{dy}{dx} + (x^2 - n^2)y = 0 \]

is known as Bessel's equation of order \( n \) and its particular solutions are called Bessel's functions.

Bessel Functions

Series solution of Bessel's equation in terms of Bessel's functions \( J_n(x) \) and \( J_{-n}(x) \) is given by:

\[ y = AJ_n(x) + BJ_{-n}(x) \]

where

\[ J_n(x) = \sum_{r=0}^{\infty} (-1)^r \frac{1}{r! \Gamma(n+r+1)} \left( \frac{x}{2} \right)^{n+2r} \]
\[ J_{-n}(x) = \sum_{r=0}^{\infty} (-1)^r \frac{1}{r! \Gamma(-n+r+1)} \left( \frac{x}{2} \right)^{-n+2r} \]

Recurrence Relations

\[ \frac{d}{dx}[x^nJ_n(x)] = x^nJ_{n-1}(x) \]
\[ \frac{d}{dx}[x^{-n}J_n(x)] = -x^{-n}J_{n+1}(x) \]
\[ J_n'(x) = J_{n-1}(x) - \frac{n}{x} J_n(x) \]
\[ J_n'(x) = -J_{n+1}(x) + \frac{n}{x} J_n(x) \]

Legendre's Equation

Another important differential equation used in problems showing spherical symmetry is Legendre's equation:

\[ (1 - x^2) \frac{d^2 y}{dx^2} - 2x \frac{dy}{dx} + n(n + 1)y = 0 \]

Legendre Polynomials

Series solution in terms of Legendre's function \( P_n(x) \) and \( Q_n(x) \) is given by:

\[ y = AP_n(x) + BQ_n(x) \]

where \( P_n(x) \) is a terminating series containing positive powers of \( x \):

\[ P_n(x) = \frac{1.3.5...(2n-1)}{n!} \left[ x^n - \frac{n(n-1)}{2(2n-1)} x^{n-2} + \frac{n(n-1)(n-2)(n-3)}{2.4.(2n-1)(2n-3)} x^{n-4} - \cdots \right] \]

Generating Function

\[ (1 - 2xz + z^2)^{-\frac{1}{2}} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} P_n(x)z^n \]

Rodrigue's Formula

\[ P_n(x) = \frac{1}{2^n n!} \frac{d^n}{dx^n} (x^2 - 1)^n \]

Recurrence Relations

\[ (n+1)P_{n+1}(x) = (2n+1)xP_n(x) - nP_{n-1}(x) \]
\[ P_n(x) = P'_{n+1}(x) - 2xP'_n(x) + P'_{n-1}(x) \]

Solved Examples

Example 1: Bessel Function Recurrence

Prove that \( \frac{d}{dx}[x^nJ_n(x)] = x^nJ_{n-1}(x) \)

Solution:

Starting with the definition:

\[ J_n(x) = \sum_{r=0}^{\infty}(-1)^r\frac{1}{r! \Gamma(n+r+1)}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{n+2r} \]

Then:

\[ x^nJ_n(x) = \sum_{r=0}^{\infty}(-1)^r\frac{1}{r! \Gamma(n+r+1)}\frac{x^{2n+2r}}{2^{n+2r}} \]

Differentiating both sides:

\[ \frac{d}{dx}[x^nJ_n(x)] = \sum_{r=0}^{\infty}(-1)^r\frac{1}{r! \Gamma(n+r+1)}\frac{2(n+r)x^{2n+2r-1}}{2^{n+2r}} \]

Using \( \Gamma(n + r + 1) = (n + r)\Gamma(n + r) \):

\[ = x^n\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}(-1)^r\frac{1}{r! \Gamma((n-1)+r+1)}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{(n-1)+2r} \]
\[ = x^nJ_{n-1}(x) \]

Example 2: Legendre Polynomial Expansion

Express \( x^3 \) in terms of Legendre polynomials.

Solution:

We know:

\[ P_0(x) = 1, \quad P_1(x) = x \]
\[ P_2(x) = \frac{1}{2}(3x^2 - 1) \Rightarrow x^2 = \frac{1}{3}(2P_2(x) + P_0(x)) \]
\[ P_3(x) = \frac{1}{2}(5x^3 - 3x) \Rightarrow x^3 = \frac{1}{5}(2P_3(x) + 3P_1(x)) \]

Therefore:

\[ x^3 = \frac{2}{5}P_3(x) + \frac{3}{5}P_1(x) \]

Example 3: Orthogonality of Bessel Functions

If \( \alpha \) and \( \beta \) be roots of the equation \( J_n(x) = 0 \), then

\[ \int_{0}^{1} x J_n(\alpha x) J_n(\beta x) dx = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } \beta \neq \alpha \\ \frac{1}{2} J_{n+1}^2 (\alpha) & \text{if } \beta = \alpha \end{cases} \]

Solution:

Given that \( \alpha \) and \( \beta \) be roots of the equation \( J_n(x) = 0 \), so \( J_n(\alpha) = J_n(\beta) = 0 \).

Consider the Bessel's equations:

\[ x^2 u'' + xu' + (\alpha^2 x^2 - n^2)u = 0 \]
\[ x^2 v'' + xv' + (\beta^2 x^2 - n^2)v = 0 \]

Solutions of these equations are respectively \( u = J_n(\alpha x) \) and \( v = J_n(\beta x) \).

Multiplying the first by \( \frac{v}{x} \) and the second by \( \frac{u}{x} \) and subtracting, we get:

\[ \frac{d}{dx} [x(u'v - uv')] + (\alpha^2 - \beta^2)uvx = 0 \]

Integrating with respect to \( x \) from 0 to 1:

\[ [x(u'v - uv')]_{0}^{1} + (\alpha^2 - \beta^2) \int_{0}^{1} uvx \, dx = 0 \]

At \( x = 1 \), \( u = J_n(\alpha) = 0 \), \( v = J_n(\beta) = 0 \), so the first term vanishes.

Thus:

\[ (\alpha^2 - \beta^2) \int_{0}^{1} xJ_n(\alpha x)J_n(\beta x) \, dx = 0 \]

If \( \beta \neq \alpha \), then the integral must be zero.

For the case \( \beta = \alpha \), we take the limit as \( \beta \to \alpha \) and apply L'Hopital's rule to get:

\[ \int_{0}^{1} xJ_n^2(\alpha x) \, dx = \frac{1}{2} J_{n+1}^2 (\alpha) \]
