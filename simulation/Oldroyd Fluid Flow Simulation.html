About Oldroyd Fluid Flow

The Oldroyd fluid model captures viscoelastic behavior in non-Newtonian fluids. Unlike Newtonian fluids, Oldroyd fluids exhibit:

  • Stress relaxation
  • Shear-thinning or shear-thickening behavior
  • Normal stress differences

Parameters α₁ and α₂ control the non-Newtonian characteristics of the fluid.

Slip Boundary Conditions

In non-Newtonian fluids like polymer melts, the no-slip boundary condition may not hold. Slip occurs when:

  • Tangential stress reaches a critical value
  • Fluid velocity at the boundary is proportional to shear rate
  • Slip coefficient γ determines the degree of slip

With slip, velocity profiles become flatter due to reduced shearing force from boundaries.

Magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) Effects

When an electrically conducting fluid flows in a magnetic field:

  • Lorentz force opposes fluid motion
  • Velocity decreases with increasing magnetic field strength
  • Flow profiles become more flattened

MHD flows have applications in metallurgy, plasma studies, and biomedical engineering.

HAM vs Numerical Solutions

The Homotopy Analysis Method (HAM) provides analytical solutions for nonlinear problems:

  • Does not require small/large parameters
  • Provides control over convergence with parameter ħ
  • Gives uniformly valid solutions

Numerical methods (finite difference) provide validation for HAM solutions.
