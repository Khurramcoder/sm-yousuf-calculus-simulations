Two laws govern every triangle drawn on a sphere. Both are derived here directly from vectors — and then verified live, on a triangle you can reshape with your own hand.

I · The setting

A triangle without
straight lines

Place three points A, B, C on a sphere of radius 1, and connect each pair by the shorter arc of the great circle through them. This is a spherical triangle — its "straight lines" are geodesics, the sphere's equivalent of straight lines.

side a = arc BC side b = arc AC side c = arc AB

Because the sphere has unit radius, an arc's length equals the angle it subtends at the center, in radians. This single fact is what makes the whole proof possible: it lets us replace lengths with angles between vectors — and angles between unit vectors are just dot products.

reading sides off the sphere

Each vertex also carries an angle — A, B, C — the angle between the two great-circle arcs that meet there, measured in the tangent plane at that point. These six quantities, three sides and three angles, are exactly what the two laws below relate to one another.
