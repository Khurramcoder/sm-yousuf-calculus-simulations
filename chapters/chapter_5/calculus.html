The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus

A unified bridge between the two central operations of analysis — differentiation and integration — brought to life through interactive simulation.

1 · The Idea in One Sentence

If we define the accumulated area under a curve $f$ from a fixed point $a$ up to a variable point $x$, then the rate at which that area grows is exactly the height of the curve at $x$. In other words, integration and differentiation are inverse operations.

$$F(x)=\int_a^x f(t)\,dt \quad\Longrightarrow\quad F'(x)=f(x)$$

This is the First Fundamental Theorem of Calculus. The Second part tells us how to evaluate a definite integral using any antiderivative:

$$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = F(b)-F(a),\qquad\text{where }F'=f.$$

4 · Formal Statements

Part I — Differentiation of an Integral

Let $f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}$ be continuous. Define

$$F(x)=\int_a^x f(t)\,dt,\qquad x\in[a,b].$$

Then $F$ is differentiable on $(a,b)$ and

$$F'(x)=f(x)\quad\text{for all }x\in(a,b).$$
Sketch of proof

For small $h>0$, $$\frac{F(x+h)-F(x)}{h}=\frac{1}{h}\int_x^{x+h}f(t)\,dt.$$ By continuity, $f(t)=f(x)+\varepsilon(t)$ with $\varepsilon(t)\to 0$ as $t\to x$. Hence the quotient equals $f(x)+\frac{1}{h}\int_x^{x+h}\varepsilon(t)\,dt\to f(x)$.

Part II — Evaluation of a Definite Integral

If $F$ is any antiderivative of a continuous $f$ on $[a,b]$ (i.e. $F'=f$), then

$$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = F(b)-F(a).$$
Why Part II follows from Part I

Let $G(x)=\int_a^x f(t)\,dt$. By Part I, $G'=f=F'$. Hence $G$ and $F$ differ by a constant $C$. Since $G(a)=0$, we have $C=-F(a)$, so $G(x)=F(x)-F(a)$. Evaluating at $x=b$ gives the result.

5 · A Worked Example

Compute $\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi} \sin x\,dx$.

An antiderivative of $\sin x$ is $F(x)=-\cos x$. By Part II:

$$\int_0^{\pi}\sin x\,dx = \bigl[-\cos x\bigr]_0^{\pi} = (-\cos\pi)-(-\cos 0)=1+1=2.$$

Check with Part I: the area function $A(x)=\int_0^x\sin t\,dt=1-\cos x$ satisfies $A'(x)=\sin x$, exactly as the theorem predicts.

6 · Why the Theorem Matters

  • It turns the limit of sums (a hard object) into evaluation of an antiderivative (an algebraic task).
  • It guarantees that every continuous function has an antiderivative — namely $x\mapsto\int_a^x f(t)\,dt$.
  • It underlies virtually every application: physics (work, flux), probability (CDFs and PDFs), engineering (signal energy), and beyond.
