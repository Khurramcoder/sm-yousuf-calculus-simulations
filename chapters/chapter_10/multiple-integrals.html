Geometric Intuition & Concepts

From 1D to Higher Dimensions

1D Integration (Length):

Integrating \(f(x) = 1\) gives the length of an interval.

2D Integration (Area):

Integrating 1 over a region \(R\) gives its area.

Double Integral (Volume):

The volume under surface \(z = f(x,y)\) above region \(R\).

Triple Integral (Mass):

Integrating density \(\rho(x,y,z)\) over 3D region \(E\) gives total mass.

Core Properties & Transformations

1. Linearity

Integrals preserve linear combinations: constants factor out, and sums split into separate integrals.

2. Domain Additivity

If region \(R\) splits into non-overlapping \(R_1\) and \(R_2\), the integral over \(R\) is the sum of integrals over each part.

3. Bounding Inequality

The integral is bounded by the minimum and maximum function values multiplied by the area of the domain.

4. Fubini's Theorem

For continuous functions, iterated integrals can be computed in either order. This enables flexible setup for easier integration.

5. Change of Variables

Transform coordinates using the Jacobian determinant to adjust the area/volume element.
