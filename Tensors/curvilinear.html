1.1

Curvilinear Coordinates

Surfaces, curves and the Jacobian condition.

r = r(u¹,u²,u³)

From straight grids to bent ones

Choose three numbers (u¹, u², u³) and demand that they fix exactly one point of space through a position vector. Holding one coordinate fixed while the others vary sweeps a coordinate surface; holding two fixed traces a coordinate curve.

The map must be invertible — every point gets one label and every label one point. The guard for that is a non-vanishing Jacobian:

DEF 1.1r(u¹,u²,u³) = x(u) x̂ + y(u) ŷ + z(u) ẑ
INVERSEuⁱ = uⁱ(x,y,z),   i = 1,2,3
REGULARJ = ∂(x,y,z)∂(u¹,u²,u³) ≠ 0

For orthogonal systems the Jacobian equals the product of scale factors, J = h₁h₂h₃ — the same factor that appears in the volume element.

1.2

Unit Vectors in a Curvilinear System

The natural basis bends; normalising it gives the unit triad.

êᵢ = (1/hᵢ) ∂r/∂uⁱ

Tangent (natural) basis

Differentiate the position vector along each coordinate curve. The three tangents form the natural basis; their lengths are the scale factors hᵢ, and dividing them out yields unit vectors.

ei = ∂r∂uⁱ,   hi = |ei|,   êi = 1hiei

In cylindrical and spherical systems the triad changes direction from point to point — êρ at φ = 0 points along x̂, at φ = π/2 along ŷ. Never drag such a vector between points without re-expressing it.

Unit triads in Cartesian components

êρ = (cosφ, sinφ, 0),   êφ = (−sinφ, cosφ, 0),   êz = ẑ
êr = (sinθcosφ, sinθsinφ, cosθ)
êθ = (cosθcosφ, cosθsinφ, −sinθ)
êφ = (−sinφ, cosφ, 0)

1.3

Arc Length, Volume & Special Coordinates

The scale factors build the metric and the volume element.

dV = h₁h₂h₃ du¹du²du³

Line and volume elements

For orthogonal systems the squared arc length is a weighted sum — the weights hᵢ² are the diagonal metric components — and the infinitesimal cell is a rectangular box with edges hᵢ du:

METRICds² = h₁²(du¹)² + h₂²(du²)² + h₃²(du³)²
VOLUMEdV = h₁h₂h₃  du¹ du² du³

That is why cylindrical integrals carry an extra ρ and spherical ones an r² sin θ — they are not memorised tricks, they are the h-products.

Special curvilinear systems

SYSTEM(u¹,u²,u³)(h₁,h₂,h₃)dV
Cartesian(x,y,z)(1,1,1)dx dy dz
Cylindrical(ρ,φ,z)(1,ρ,1)ρ dρ dφ dz
Spherical(r,θ,φ)(1,r,r sinθ)r² sinθ dr dθ dφ
Parabolic(σ,τ,z)(√(σ²+τ²), √(σ²+τ²), 1)(σ²+τ²) dσ dτ dz

1.4

Contravariant & Covariant Vectors

One geometric arrow, two bookkeeping systems.

V = Vⁱeᵢ = Vᵢeⁱ

Two bases, two component sets

Expand the arrow on the natural basis and the components are contravariant (upper index); expand it on the dual basis — defined by eⁱ·eⱼ = δⁱⱼ — and they are covariant (lower index). The metric raises and lowers:

V = Vⁱei = Vᵢei
Vᵢ = gijVʲ,   Vⁱ = gijVⱼ,   gij = ei·ej

Contravariant components are the parallelogram coordinates along the axes; covariant components are orthogonal projections onto them. They coincide only for orthonormal bases.

1.5 · Coordinate transformations

Under x → x′ the chain rule splits the world cleanly: components with upper indices transform with ∂x′/∂x (like displacements), components with lower indices with ∂x/∂x′ (like gradients):

V′ⁱ = ∂x′ⁱ∂xʲVʲ    V′ᵢ = ∂xʲ∂x′ⁱVⱼ
e′i = ∂xʲ∂x′ⁱej   (basis transforms oppositely — hence “contra”)

1.7

The Kronecker Delta

The identity tensor: it substitutes indices and checks the books.

δⁱⱼ = eⁱ·eⱼ

Definition & duties

δij = { 1 if i = j ;  0 otherwise }
δijVʲ = Vⁱ  ·  gikgkj = δij  ·  ei·ej = δij

It is the duality handshake: the dual basis is precisely the set of vectors whose dot products with the natural basis give δ. It also certifies inverse transformations: (∂x′ⁱ/∂x)(∂xᵏ/∂x′ʲ) = δⁱⱼ — verified live with your matrices in SIM 03.

Why it is a (1,1) tensor

Transform δ with the mixed-tensor rule and the two Jacobians collapse by the chain rule, leaving δ unchanged — its components are identical in every coordinate system, which is exactly what the SIM 03 δ-matrix displays (1.00 on the diagonal, 0.00 elsewhere).

δ′ij = ∂x′ⁱ∂xᵃ∂xᵇ∂x′ʲδab = ∂x′ⁱ∂x′ʲ = δij

Practical habits: δ contracts (substitutes) indices, raises/lowers nothing, and turns “same index twice” sums into single terms — δⁱⱼAⁱBʲ = A·B in orthonormal frames.

Chapter wrap-up

• Coordinates are labels; the Jacobian ≠ 0 keeps them honest.  • Bases come from tangents: eᵢ = ∂r/∂uⁱ, units from hᵢ.  • ds² and dV are built from the hᵢ — never memorise ρ and r² sinθ, derive them.  • Upper = contravariant (parallelogram), lower = covariant (projection); the metric translates.  • Tensors are transformation behaviours; δⁱⱼ is the invariant (1,1) identity.
