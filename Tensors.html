Section 01 · Rank-2 anatomy

Symmetric & Skew-Symmetric Tensors

Every rank-2 tensor splits, uniquely, into a part that stretches and a part that rotates. The two plates below are that theorem, made visible.

Definition 1.1Symmetry

A rank-2 tensor Tij is symmetric if Tij = Tji and skew-symmetric (antisymmetric) if Tij = −Tji.

Setting i = j in the skew condition gives Tii = −Tii ⇒ Tii = 0: every diagonal entry of a skew tensor vanishes, so a skew tensor in n dimensions carries only n(n−1)/2 independent components, against n(n+1)/2 for a symmetric one.

Theorem 1.2Canonical decomposition

Every rank-2 tensor splits uniquely as symmetric + skew:

Tij = T(ij) + T[ij],   T(ij) := 12(Tij+Tji),   T[ij] := 12(Tij−Tji)

Proof. Round brackets symmetrise, square brackets antisymmetrise by construction; adding the two definitions telescopes to Tij. For uniqueness, suppose T = S+K = S′+K′. Then D := S−S′ = K′−K is simultaneously symmetric and skew, so Dij = Dji = −Dji ⇒ D = 0. ∎

The dimensions agree: n(n+1)/2 + n(n−1)/2 = n2, the full count of a rank-2 tensor.

Worked ExampleDecompose by hand

Take 452163−208 (3 columns). Symmetric part — average with the transpose:

430361.501.58  +  022−201.5−2−1.50

and their sum reproduces T exactly. Every numerical simulation in this atlas obeys the same identity to machine precision.

Theorem 1.3Skew tensors ↔ axial vectors in ℝ³

In three dimensions the space of skew tensors is 3-dimensional, exactly like vectors. The bridge is the Levi-Civita symbol εijk:

Kij = −εijkωk,    ωk = −12εkijKij

so that acting with K is the cross product: (Kx)i = (ω × x)i. In components, Kij is the matrix shown in Plate II. Consequently det K = 0 and rank K = 2 (when ω ≠ 0): its kernel is the axis ω itself.

Remark 1.4What each part does to space
  • Symmetric: stretches along an orthogonal frame of principal directions (Plate I). Quadratic form xiAijxj — energy, inertia, stress.
  • Skew: infinitesimal rigid rotation (Plate II); vorticity ω = 12∇×v in fluid mechanics.
  • The symmetric part alone is felt by quadratic forms: xiTijxj = xiT(ij)xj, since the skew part cancels against itself.
Proposition 1.5Symmetry survives coordinate changes

If Tij transforms covariantly, T′ij = ∂xp∂x′i∂xq∂x′jTpq, then swapping i↔j merely swaps the dummy labels p,q — so symmetry (and skew-symmetry) is a coordinate-independent property, not an artefact of one frame.

Section 02 · The vector space of tensors

Addition & Subtraction of Tensors

Tensors of a fixed type form a vector space: they add componentwise, they negate, they cancel. Plate III overlays three ellipsoids so you can watch A+B happen.

Definition 2.1Sum and difference

For two tensors of the same valence and the same variance type, addition and subtraction are componentwise:

(A ± B)ij…pq… = Aij…pq… ± Bij…pq…

The expression A+B is undefined if A and B have different index structures — you may not add a vector to a rank-2 tensor, nor a covariant pair to a mixed pair (without first using the metric of §07).

Theorem 2.2Closure: the sum is a tensor

Proof. Both terms obey the same linear transformation law, e.g. for type (0,2):

(A+B)′ij = ∂xp∂x′i∂xq∂x′j(Apq+Bpq) = ∂xp∂x′i∂xq∂x′j(A+B)pq

because the Jacobian factors distribute. Hence tensors of a fixed type form a vector space of dimension nr+s: addition is commutative and associative, there is a zero tensor 0, and every tensor has the additive inverse −B, which is what subtraction means: A−B := A+(−B). ∎

Remark 2.3What is and isn't linear
  • The trace is linear: tr(A±B) = tr A ± tr B — verify with the chips beside Plate III.
  • The determinant is not: det(A+B) ≠ det A + det B in general.
  • There is no “division” of tensors; the quotient law of §03 is the closest legitimate substitute.

Outer Product, Inner Product & the Quotient Law

Three operations manufacture tensors: juxtaposition (outer), contraction (inner), and implication (the quotient law — if it acts like a tensor on everything, it is one).

Definition 3.1Outer (tensor) product

Multiply components, concatenate indices:

(A ⊗ B)i…mj…q = Ai…j… · Bm…q…

Ranks add. The dyad of two vectors, (a⊗b)ij = aibj, is the rank-1 matrix of Plate IV. The outer product is not commutative: a⊗b ≠ b⊗a, but (a⊗b)T = b⊗a. That it produces a genuine tensor follows because products of Jacobian factors are Jacobian factors.

Definition 3.2Contraction & inner product

Contraction sets one upper index equal to one lower index and sums — rank drops by two: Cil = Aijjl. The inner product of two tensors is the contraction of their outer product; the archetypes:

  • a·b = aibi — scalar (Plate V);
  • Cik = AijBjk — matrix multiplication (§04);
  • A:B = AijBij — full (double) contraction, the Frobenius pairing.

Theorem (contraction). Any contraction of a tensor is a tensor. Proof sketch: the paired Jacobians collapse, ∂x′j∂xk∂xk∂x′j = δjj, by the chain rule — exactly one factor of the transformation law disappears for each contraction. ∎

Theorem 3.3The quotient law

Let Tij be a set of n2 functions in every coordinate system, and suppose that for every vector x the quantities yi = Tijxj are the components of a vector. Then T is a tensor of type (1,1).

Proof. In a primed frame, two expressions for y′p hold. First, because y is a vector: y′p = ∂x′p∂xiTijxj = ∂x′p∂xi∂xj∂x′qTijx′q. Second, by the hypothesis applied in the primed frame: y′p = T′pqx′q. Subtracting, (T′pq − ∂x′p/∂xi · ∂xj/∂x′q Tij) x′q = 0 for all x′, hence the bracket vanishes — the tensor law itself. ∎

Corollaries 3.4Uses of the law
  • Kronecker delta: δijxj = xi is a vector for all x, so δ is a (1,1) tensor — in every coordinate system.
  • Metric from a line element: if gijdxidxj is a scalar for all displacements, then gij is a (0,2) tensor (the symmetric part; see §05).
  • Inverse map: if Aijxj and its inverse output vectors for all inputs, the inverse array is a tensor too.
  • Variants exist for higher-rank testers: contracting with an arbitrary tensor and getting a tensor implies tensoriality of the quotient.

Section 04 · The familiar, promoted

Matrix Algebra as Tensor Algebra

A matrix is the component array of a mixed (1,1) tensor — a linear map. Multiplication is composition, the trace is a contraction, and the determinant is the volume scale factor.

A = rotation about e₃ by α · B = shear x ↦ x + k·y
AB ≠ BA det A = 1det B = 1det(AB) = 1
parallelepiped spanned by M·e₁, M·e₂, M·e₃
det M = 0volume = 0shear leaves det unchanged
DictionaryMatrix ↔ tensor
  • Matrix entries ↔ components of a mixed tensor Aij (a linear map V→V).
  • Cik = AijBjk — inner product of (1,1) tensors = matrix multiplication = composition of maps.
  • tr A = Aii — contraction; a true invariant.
  • I ↔ δij, the identity tensor in all coordinates.
Theorem 4.1Invariants under change of basis

Under x′ = Λx a mixed tensor transforms by similarity, A′ = ΛAΛ−1. Therefore

tr A′ = tr(ΛAΛ−1) = tr A,   det A′ = det Λ · det A · det Λ−1 = det A

so trace, determinant, eigenvalues and every coefficient of the characteristic polynomial det(A−λδ) = 0 are coordinate-free. By Cayley–Hamilton, A satisfies its own characteristic polynomial. The inverse, when det A ≠ 0, is (A−1)ij = cofactor(A)ijdet A and is itself a tensor (quotient law).

Worked ExampleNon-commutativity, numerically

Let A = 1201 (shear) and B = 0110 (swap). Then

2110 = AB   ≠   BA = 0112

yet det(AB) = det A · det B = (−1)(−1)… both determinants multiply consistently: det is a homomorphism even though multiplication is not commutative.

Caution 4.2The transpose is not a tensor operation

For a mixed tensor, “AT” has no invariant meaning: swapping an upper and a lower index mixes variance types. Transpose becomes meaningful only after a metric identifies indices — which is precisely the content of §07. What is always meaningful: trace, determinant, symmetrisation of like-type index pairs, and contraction.

Section 05 · Measuring with tensors

The Line Element & Metric Tensors

One symmetric (0,2) tensor encodes every measurement a space allows: lengths, angles, areas, volumes. It is read directly off the line element ds².

Definition 5.1Line element and metric

The squared infinitesimal distance between neighbouring points is a quadratic form in the coordinate differentials:

ds2 = gij dxi dxj

The coefficients form a symmetric covariant rank-2 tensor gij — the metric. Reading examples off: Euclidean plane g = diag(1,1); polar coordinates ds2 = dr2 + r2dφ2 so gφφ = r2; the unit sphere ds2 = dθ2 + sin2θ dφ2 (Plate IX); and in relativity the Minkowski metric diag(−1,1,1,1), which is merely non-degenerate rather than positive-definite.

Theorem 5.2g is a tensor; what it measures

Since ds2 is a geometric scalar and dxi is a vector, the quotient law (§03) forces gij to be a (0,2) tensor. Everything measurable follows:

  • Length of a curve: L = ∫ √(gij ẋiẋj) dt
  • Angle: cos ϑ = gijuivj ⁄ (|u| |v|)
  • Volume: dV = √g dx1…dxn, where g := det(gij) — on the sphere √g = sin θ.

Coordinates lie: equal coordinate steps are not equal distances. The metric is the correction table.

Remark 5.3Why g must be symmetric

In gijdxidxj, only the symmetric part of g survives (Remark 1.4), so one takes gij = gji without loss. Non-degeneracy (det g ≠ 0) guarantees the inverse gij exists — the “reciprocal tensor” of the next section.

Example 5.4A curved distance

On the unit sphere at colatitude θ = 60°, a coordinate step dφ = 0.2 has true length ds = sin 60° · 0.2 ≈ 0.173, while near the pole (θ = 5°) the same step is only ≈ 0.017 — ten times shorter. Reproduce both numbers with the Plate IX slider.

Section 06 · Two bases, two component systems

Conjugate / Reciprocal Tensors

Every basis has a shadow basis built from it, and the two measure components in complementary ways. Contravariant components climb against the basis; covariant components flow with it.

Definition 6.1Reciprocal basis

Given a basis ei, its reciprocal (conjugate) basis ei is defined by the duality condition

ei · ej = δij

In terms of the metric, ei = gijej, and the check is one line: ei·ej = gikgkj = δij. Geometrically in the plane, e1 is perpendicular to e2 — exactly what Plate XI draws.

Theorem 6.2Two component systems of one vector

The same vector expands in either basis:

v = viei = viei,   vi = ei·v,   vi = ei·v,   vi = gijvj

The contravariant components vi come from projecting along parallels to the basis (the parallelogram); the covariant components vi come from perpendicular projection. Under a change of coordinates they transform in opposite (contra- vs co-variant) ways, so that v itself is untouched.

Definition 6.3The reciprocal (contravariant) metric tensor

The matrix inverse of gij is itself a tensor, of type (2,0):

gikgkj = δij

Classical texts call gij the contravariant or reciprocal tensor of gij. Proof it transforms correctly: invert both sides of the covariant law and use (J−1)−1 = J. Raising with gij generalises to any tensor: Aij = girgjsArs — the reciprocal associate of §07.

MnemonicCo- vs contra-
  • Covariant indices transform with the Jacobian ∂x/∂x′ — like basis vectors and gradients.
  • Contravariant indices transform against it, with the inverse — like displacements and velocities.
  • One up + one down, summed = invariant. Every equation in this atlas obeys the balance rule.
Example 6.4Reading Plate XI

With e1 = (1,0), e2 = (cos α, sin α), the covariant metric is gij = 1cc1 with c = cos α, and its reciprocal gij = 1sin²α1−c−c1. As α → 0 the bases blow up — a degenerate basis has no reciprocal, just as a singular matrix has no inverse.

Section 07 · The metric as translator

Associated Tensors

Raising and lowering indices: every tensor owns a family of associates, one for each pattern of up/down indices, and the metric is the dictionary between them.

Definition 7.1Associates by raising and lowering

Given Aij and the metric pair gij, gij, its associated tensors are obtained one index at a time:

Aij = gikAkj,   Aij = girgjsArs,   Aij = gikgjlAkl

Raising then lowering (or conversely) is the identity, since gikgkj = δij: the associates are one tensor seen through different index placements, not different tensors. For the metric itself the chain collapses beautifully: gij → gij = δij → gij.

Theorem 7.2The musical isomorphisms

A non-degenerate metric gives a canonical bijection between vectors and covectors:

♭ : vi ↦ vi = gijvj    ♯ : ωi ↦ ωi = gijωj

Proof of isomorphism: linearity is obvious; injectivity follows because gijvj = 0 for all i implies v = 0 by non-degeneracy; equal finite dimensions finish it. ∎

Key point: without a metric there is no natural way to convert a vector into a covector. The metric is precisely the extra structure that authorises the identification that Euclidean intuition takes for granted.

Physical InterludeWhy associates matter
  • Gradient: df = (∂f/∂xi)dxi is naturally a covector; the “grad” vector of vector calculus is its sharp: (grad f)i = gij∂jf.
  • Continuum mechanics: stress and strain are swapped between covariant, mixed and contravariant forms to build invariants (energy density σijεij).
  • Relativity: pi = gijpj turns four-velocity into four-momentum co-components; Maxwell's Fij and Fij are associates whose mixing encodes E ↔ B under boosts.
